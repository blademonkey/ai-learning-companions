Part 1: Two kinds of question

The GraphRAG paper separates lookup questions from global (sensemaking) questions about a whole collection.research claim, Edge et al. 2024

The paper says conventional RAG fails on global questions because they are summarization tasks, not retrieval tasks.research claim, Edge et al. 2024

Putting everything on the desk is not a fix: the desk is limited, and models use the middle of long input less well in the 2023 study we cited.research, Liu et al. 2023, models of that time

Part 2: What a graph is

NodesThings: people, places, ideas
EdgesRelationships between things

In the paper, nodes are key entities and edges are relationships.research description, Edge et al.

A graph makes connections explicit. That is our own reasoning, not a finding.our interpretation

A wiki's page links can be read as a graph. That is our framing.our interpretation

Part 3: How a model builds a graph from text

In the paper, text is split into chunks and a model extracts entities and relationships from each, using prompts tailored to the data.research description

Repeated relationships become edge weights. The paper used exact string matching to reconcile names and says duplicates are mostly clustered together later.research description, Edge et al. 3.1.3

A wrong extraction is stored the same way as a right one. That follows from the pipeline.our interpretation

Graph building and community summaries happen at indexing time, before any question. Answering happens at query time.research description, Edge et al. Figure 1

This is the same compile-ahead instinct as the LLM wiki.our interpretation

Part 5: What the evidence shows

Two datasets: podcast transcripts (about 1 million tokens) and news articles (about 1.7 million). A model generated 125 questions per dataset; a model judged the answers.research method, Edge et al.

Comprehensiveness win rates over vector RAG: 72 to 83 percent (podcast) and 72 to 80 percent (news).research result, author-run, GPT-4, 2024

Vector RAG gave the most direct answers in every comparison; empowerment results were mixed.research result, Edge et al.

Top-level community summaries needed 9 to 43 times fewer tokens per query than summarizing the source texts.research result, Edge et al. Table 2 caption

Outside work is more cautious: a 2025 benchmark study found RAG and GraphRAG have distinct strengths by task and that combining them helped.research claim, arXiv 2502.11371, abstract and opening text read, not full experiments

A second 2025 paper says common GraphRAG evaluation has flaws (unrelated questions, judge bias) and that with these corrected, three GraphRAG methods' gains are much more moderate; its authors say their framework may still have flaws.research claim, arXiv 2506.06331, abstract and opening text read, not full experiments

GraphRAG-Bench opens by noting recent studies report GraphRAG frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on many real-world tasks.research claim, arXiv 2506.05690, abstract and opening text read, not full experiments

Treat the 72 to 83 percent figures as the first author-run result, not a settled advantage.our interpretation

Part 6: Where graphs fail and what they cost

Our reasoning: extraction errors, name merging, missing edges, staleness, opacity.our interpretation

From the paper: indexing costs model calls over every chunk plus community summaries; specific examples, quotes and citations can be lost.research description

Part 7 and 8: Choosing, and back to the archive

Lookups: retrieval. Modest pile plus synthesis: a compiled wiki. Whole-collection questions on a big archive: trial a graph on a small slice you know well.our recommendation

Check the graph against material you can verify before trusting it on the full archive.our recommendation

The one idea

Some questions are about one fact and some are about the shape of a whole collection. Retrieval fetches a few pieces and cannot see the whole. A knowledge graph stores things and their relationships. GraphRAG, from Microsoft researchers, has a model build a graph from text, group it into communities, pre-write a summary of each community, and answer a global question by combining those summaries. Its authors report that its answers were judged more comprehensive and diverse on global questions, and less direct, on two datasets of 1 to 2 million tokens, using a model as the judge.

Key terms

Node. A thing in a graph: a person, place, organization or idea.Edge et al.

Edge. A relationship between two nodes.Edge et al.

Knowledge graph. Nodes and edges extracted from documents. In GraphRAG, built by a model.Edge et al.

Community. A group of nodes more connected to each other than to the rest. GraphRAG finds a hierarchy of them.Edge et al.

Global question. A question about the whole collection, such as main themes. Also called a sensemaking question.Edge et al.

Vector RAG. Conventional retrieval: fetch the pieces whose numeric fingerprints are closest to the question.Edge et al.

Indexing time and query time. Graph building happens before questions; answering happens after.Edge et al. Figure 1

Worked scenario (invented)

A local history society (invented) has 3,000 letters. 'What did the mill owner write about the flood?' suits retrieval. 'Who bridged the town's groups?' is a global question. A careful volunteer builds a graph on 100 letters she knows well, checks that the people she knows are central actually show up, and only then runs the full archive.invented illustration

Decision card

  1. Is the question a lookup or about the whole collection?
  2. Is the collection too big to read?
  3. Can you check the output against material you know?
  4. Who would notice a wrong connection?
  5. What did indexing cost, and what happens when documents change?

Counterpoints and limits

  • The evidence is one paper, by the method's authors, with a model as judge and model-generated questions.Edge et al.
  • Study models were GPT-4-era (2024); the percentages show the idea can work, not what you will get today.Edge et al.
  • The paper reports vector RAG was most direct in every comparison.Edge et al.
  • Whether duplicate names matter for a 'who is most connected' question was not tested in the passages we read.our interpretation
  • Independent work (2025) reports more moderate gains; we read those abstracts only, not the papers.source limit

Try it yourself

Take 20 pages you know well. Ask a model to list the people, places and relationships in each. Compare with what you know. Count what it got wrong or missed. That is a one-hour version of the check in the episode.

Quiz

  1. What kind of question can retrieval not answer well, according to the GraphRAG paper?
    AnswerGlobal questions about a whole corpus, because they are summarization tasks rather than retrieval tasks.
  2. What are the two stages of GraphRAG's index?
    AnswerFirst an entity knowledge graph from the documents, then pre-generated summaries of communities of closely related entities.
  3. Name one thing the paper reports GraphRAG did worse than vector RAG.
    AnswerDirectness: vector RAG gave the most direct answers in every comparison.
  4. Why is the judge a limit?
    AnswerBoth the questions and the judging were done by models, so the test shows which answers a model judge preferred, not whether they were true.
