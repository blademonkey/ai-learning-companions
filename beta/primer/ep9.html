Episode 9: Testing and validating language models

A number without its method is not evidence. Ask what was measured, on which questions, how many times, and judged by whom.

Cold open: a demo with five questions

A city parking assistant (invented) passes five hand-picked questions, one try each, judged by its builders. No unanswerable question, no rewording, no planted instruction.invented illustration

Most studies cited were run on 2020-2024 models. Numbers are what was measured then, not today's behavior.stated limit

Part 1: Three things called 'evals'

OpenAI's guide: evals can mean public benchmarks, standard scores, or your own tests; the guide is about the third.vendor guidance

Anthropic: an eval gives an AI system an input and applies grading logic to the output; task, trial, grader.vendor definitions

A process framework such as CMMI describes how an organization manages work; it does not test whether a model's answer is right.our distinction

Part 2: Why a score alone misleads

HELM measures 7 metrics across 16 core scenarios (87.5 percent of combinations) and declares what is missing.research

CheckList (2020): held-out accuracy often overestimates performance; users made about twice as many tests and found about three times as many bugs.research

Contamination (test data in training data) is described as prevalent and challenging; leaderboards show inconsistencies and poor separability among top models.research

A 2025 survey of 50 detection papers reports detection methods on three assumptions can perform like random guessing on pretraining data. Keeping your own test private is our inference.research claim, abstract and opening text read, not full experiments; our interpretation

Part 3: Build the test from real cases

Task-specific evals reflecting real distributions; avoid 'vibe-based' evals; calibrate automated scoring with humans.vendor guidance, OpenAI

Write unambiguous tasks; capability evals ask what it can do, regression evals ask whether it still does.vendor guidance, Anthropic

Keep the list private and include questions the documents cannot answer.our recommendation

Part 4: Check facts claim by claim

TruthfulQA: 817 questions in 38 categories; best model 58 percent truthful vs 94 percent human; largest models generally least truthful, for the models of that time.research

Caution: the original multiple-choice format was shown gameable (79.6 percent by a decision tree); the authors released a new version and say old results are probably still valid. The 58 and 94 percent figures are from human-judged generation.third-party analysis and authors' response, read in part

FActScore: score the share of atomic facts supported. ChatGPT sentences mixed true and false in about 40 percent of cases; scores 42, 58, 71 percent for three systems on biographies; ChatGPT 80 to 16 percent as people got rarer.research result, 2023 models

Part 5: Does the answer hold up?

SelfCheckGPT: sample several answers; made-up details tend to vary between samples. A consistently wrong answer would pass (our reasoning).research method; our interpretation

Sclar et al.: formatting changes alone moved accuracy up to 76 points on LLaMA-2-13B few-shot; report a range.research result

Counterpoint (2025): across 7 models, 6 benchmarks and 12 templates, much measured prompt sensitivity came from scoring methods like log-likelihood and rigid matching; judge-based scoring cut variance.research claim, Flaw or Artifact, EMNLP 2025, abstract and opening text read, not full experiments

Part 6: Systems, not just models

Ragas scores retrieval relevance, faithful use of passages, and answer quality, without human reference answers.research description

A 2024 telecom-domain study reported little derivation detail for its numbers and few metrics scoring higher for correct retrieval; one domain, abstract and opening text read, not full experiments. Check such metrics against hand grading.research claim, arXiv 2407.12873

Grade the outcome, not the path; make graders hard to cheat; run each task several times.vendor guidance

tau-bench: 61 percent retail, 35 percent airline on one try; about 25 percent retail over eight tries.research result, 2024

Part 7: A model as the judge

GPT-4 as judge agreed with human preferences over 80 percent, similar to human-human agreement.research result, 2023

Position consistency when answer order was swapped: GPT-4 65 percent, others lower. Verbosity and math-grading weaknesses were tested. Self-enhancement could not be determined from their data.research result, Zheng et al.

Hand-grade a sample and swap order before trusting a judge.our recommendation

Part 8: Try to break it

Red teaming with a model: tens of thousands of offensive replies found in a 280B chatbot; the classifier is itself a limit.research result, 2022

Plant an instruction in a retrieved page. A pass means 'not broken by this attempt'.our recommendation; OWASP

Part 9 to 11: How many questions, what tests cannot show, back to the city

Error Bars paper: report standard error and number of questions, cluster-adjust, compare on the same questions, use power analysis. It gives formulas, not a magic number.research method

Testing can find problems; it cannot prove their absence.our statement

Reference view: written for reading and scanning, in the same order as the script.

The one idea

Testing a language model is measurement. Your own test, built from real and hard cases and kept private, answers your question; public benchmarks answer a different one. Check facts claim by claim, repeat each question and reword it, score retrieval and answers separately, run agent tasks several times, and calibrate any model used as a judge against a hand-graded sample. Try to break the system, including with planted instructions. Report ranges and question counts. A test can find problems and cannot prove there are none.

Key terms

Eval. A test for an AI system: an input plus grading logic applied to the output.Anthropic

Task, trial, grader. One test; one attempt at it; the logic that scores it.Anthropic

Contamination. Test questions leaking into training data.Jacovi et al.

Atomic fact. A single small statement checked against a source.FActScore

Model as judge. Using a language model to grade answers.Zheng et al.

Capability vs regression eval. What can it do vs does it still do what it did.Anthropic

pass^k. Probability that all k trials succeed.tau-bench; Anthropic

Red teaming. Deliberately probing for harmful or rule-breaking behavior.Perez et al.

Standard error. A measure of uncertainty in a score from a finite set of questions.Miller 2024

Head to head

GraderStrengthsWeaknesses
Code (string match, outcome check)Fast, cheap, objective, reproducibleBrittle to valid variations
Model as judgeFlexible, scalable, handles open-ended answersNon-deterministic, costlier, biases; needs human calibration
HumanClosest to what people wantSlow and expensive (our reasoning)

Worked scenario (invented)

A city parking assistant (invented) answers from the parking code. A careful team collects real resident questions with expected answers and supporting sections, adds questions the code cannot answer, asks each several ways and several times, scores retrieval and answers separately, hand-checks a sample of long answers claim by claim, checks any model judge against hand grades with answer order swapped, plants an instruction in a retrieved page, and reports scores with a range and the number of questions. It re-runs the suite whenever the model, documents or prompt change.invented illustration

Decision card

  1. What exactly was measured, on which questions?
  2. How many times was each run, and how much did rewording move it?
  3. Who or what judged, and was that checked against people?
  4. Is the test private and does it include unanswerable cases?
  5. How many questions, and what is the range?
  6. When does the suite re-run?

Counterpoints and limits

  • For HELM, CheckList, contamination papers, TruthfulQA, SelfCheckGPT, Sclar, Ragas and Perez, only the figures spoken were checked against the full text; the rest of those papers was not read.source limit
  • OpenAI and Anthropic guidance is from vendors that sell models.vendor

  • We found no source giving a minimum number of test questions.source limit

Try it yourself

Write 10 real questions your documents should answer and 3 they cannot. Ask a chatbot with your documents each one twice, worded two ways. Count how often the answers agree. That count is your first measurement.

Quiz

  1. Name the three things people mean by 'evals' in OpenAI's guide.
    AnswerIndustry benchmarks, standard numerical scores, and your own tests of your application.
  2. What does pass^k tell you that a single success rate does not?
    AnswerHow often the system succeeds every time across k tries, which is what users of a reliable system need.
  3. Name two documented weaknesses of a model used as a judge.
    AnswerPosition bias (verdict changes when answer order swaps) and difficulty grading math and reasoning. Verbosity was also tested.
  4. Does a passed red-team test show the system is safe?
    AnswerNo. It shows it was not broken by those attempts.
